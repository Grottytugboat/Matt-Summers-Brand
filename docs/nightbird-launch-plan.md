# Nightbird, introduced properly

A proposed 30-day creator pilot for Matt and Tim to shape together.

This is a planning demonstration. No creators have been hired, no work commissioned, no media booked and no campaign launched. All timelines are relative to an agreed start date. Prices, targets and responsibilities require agreement. The storyboard images are existing brand visualisations, not submitted creator videos.

## The opportunity

Matt identified Nightbird as a key first product and described an ambition to coordinate creators across several brands, with a clear path from content to customer action. He also raised the practical challenge of shipping individual bottles and protecting the economics.

Start with one brand and a manageable creative test. Nightbird’s gold geometric artwork, bird signature and clear bottle give the first stories a specific visual identity. Use those verifiable details instead of inventing tasting notes, distillation claims or brand history.

The supplied artwork identifies Nightbird as vodka, 40% alc/vol, 700 mL. Matt must confirm the current production bottle, approved product facts, stock and availability before release.

## What the pilot should prove

Test which expression of Nightbird earns meaningful interest from an adult audience, then decide the next investment using evidence.

Before starting, agree:

- The adult audience and relevant service area.
- The main channel and approved placements.
- What counts as a qualified expression of interest.
- A spending ceiling and agreed target, informed by costs and realistic reach.
- The evidence that would support extending, refining or pausing the pilot.

The current site does not accept orders or enquiries. A real launch-interest flow must be implemented and tested before sending traffic with a sign-up objective. Revenue and return-on-ad-spend reporting remain unavailable while ecommerce is paused.

## The proposed 30 days

### Week 1 — Set the direction

Approve the objective, audience, budget ceiling and three creative treatments. Confirm sample stock, single-bottle packaging, freight costs and the contribution remaining after fulfilment. Review creator candidates against the three slots below. No outreach or booking is implied by this plan.

### Week 2 — Make the first stories

Agree creator scopes, fees, revisions and usage permissions before production. Deliver samples and commission the approved work. Review three first cuts and alternate openings. In parallel, connect and test the real adult interest journey, consent handling and measurement.

### Week 3 — Release with intention

Release only after Matt approves the finished work, captions, rights and placement. Use a unique tracked link for each concept. If paid media is approved, use comparable audiences, formats, placements and observation periods. Record differences that make comparisons less reliable.

### Week 4 — Learn, then decide

Reconcile actual costs. Compare attention, qualified visits and valid interest records. Document what the sample can and cannot establish. Recommend one creative to refine, one assumption to retest and one thing to stop. Matt and Tim agree the next step by Day 30.

## Three creator slots

These describe capabilities to source, not people already selected. Fees are unquoted and all audience evidence and rights remain to be verified.

| Slot | Creative focus | Proposed deliverables |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 — Design & detail | Restrained product storytelling; glass, geometry and light | One 20-second vertical film, one alternate opening, three still frames |
| 02 — Considered hosting | Adult table styling, food, water and visual atmosphere | One 25-second vertical film, one alternate opening, three still frames |
| 03 — Cocktail craft | An adult bartender or educator showing measured preparation | One 30-second vertical film, one alternate opening, three still frames |

Agree deliverables, production dates, review rounds, organic and paid usage, territory, term, music/talent rights and any creator-account advertising in writing. Keep creator fees, sample costs and media spend visible as separate decisions.

## Creative concept 01 — The gold is in the detail

**Opening:** “A bird. A line. A different perspective.”

**Treatment:** Twenty seconds of deliberate close-ups: light reveals the gold bird, travels across the geometric artwork, then catches the faceted stopper. End on the complete bottle with the real label readable.

**Draft voiceover:** “A bird in flight. Gold lines. Clear glass. Some things are worth a closer look. Meet Nightbird Vodka.”

**Draft caption:** “A closer look at Nightbird. Clear glass, gold geometry and a signature in flight. Discover the bottle. 18+ · Enjoy responsibly.”

**Action:** Discover Nightbird.

**Learning question:** Does the distinct bottle design earn deeper product interest?

## Creative concept 02 — A considered table

**Opening:** “The table, in five details.”

**Treatment:** Twenty-five seconds of linen, glassware, food and a water carafe, followed by Nightbird as one considered element in the setting. Reveal the table without a toast, drinking montage or suggestion that alcohol changes the social experience.

**Draft voiceover:** “Linen. Glass. Something to share at the table. Water within reach. And a little gold in the detail. Nightbird Vodka.”

**Draft caption:** “A table study in texture, glass and gold. Featuring Nightbird Vodka. Explore the details. 18+ · Enjoy responsibly.”

**Action:** Explore the Nightbird world.

**Learning question:** Does the hosting context make the product more relevant to the intended adult audience?

## Creative concept 03 — The considered serve

**Opening:** “Good preparation starts with a measure.”

**Treatment:** Thirty seconds with an adult bartender: set the glass and jigger, measure the agreed serve, add ice and the approved mixer, stir and finish with the agreed garnish. Keep the emphasis on preparation. Matt must approve the actual recipe and quantities before filming.

**Draft voiceover:** “A chilled glass. Fresh ice. A measured pour. Your chosen mixer. Keep the preparation simple and let the details speak. Nightbird Vodka.”

**Draft caption:** “A measured approach to the Nightbird serve. Recipe and quantities to be approved before filming. Discover Nightbird. 18+ · Enjoy responsibly.” The recipe note is internal draft copy and must be replaced before publication.

**Action:** Discover the Nightbird serve.

**Learning question:** Does a practical introduction to the serve produce more useful product exploration?

## The release checkpoint

Before release, review the finished asset, caption, advertising disclosure, product claims, permissions and adult placement together. Confirm creator eligibility and audience evidence against the current ABAC and platform requirements; apply available age restrictions. Avoid unsubstantiated claims or depictions of alcohol as a route to social success. Matt gives final brand approval. Seek external pre-vetting where appropriate to the final treatment.

The reference material is the [ABAC digital marketing guidance](https://www.abac.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Best-Practice-for-Responsible-Digital-Marketing-4-6-25.pdf) and [ABAC Code and review process](https://abac.org.au/about/thecode/). This plan is a creative proposal, not clearance of future content.

## Budget to agree

No amounts are assumed. Add estimates to the demonstration, obtain quotes, then approve the actual scope.

1. **Creator production:** content fees, revisions and usage rights.
2. **Paid media:** optional test spend under an approved ceiling.
3. **Samples and freight:** product, protective packaging and delivery.
4. **Landing and measurement:** landing experience, capture, consent and reporting setup.
5. **Contingency:** an agreed reserve for approved changes.

For future sales, calculate the contribution remaining after product cost, packaging, freight subsidy, fees and acquisition cost. Do not infer commercial viability from attention or sign-ups alone.

## What to measure

| Measure | Source needed | Current state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creative attention | Platform viewing/completion reports | No content published |
| Qualified landing visits | Consented analytics and creative-specific links | Tracking not connected |
| Qualified launch interest | Real form, consent and record destination | Blocked until capture exists |
| Cost per valid interest | Actual costs and deduplicated valid records | Unavailable |
| Useful questions and feedback | Approved comments and consented enquiries | No live responses |
| Sales and contribution | Order and cost systems | Ecommerce not enabled |

Define qualified interest before the test: an adult, consented, unique, non-test record meeting the agreed service-area and interest criteria. Report paid-media cost per valid record separately from total-pilot cost per valid record. Where attribution is incomplete, say so. Set targets together; do not manufacture performance for the demonstration.

## Proposed responsibilities

**Matt:** Product knowledge, stock, fulfilment and costs; final approval of budget, creative and the decision to proceed.

**Tim:** Brand experience, briefs, campaign coordination, content reviews, measurement setup and useful reporting, within an agreed engagement.

**Selected creators:** Approved deliverables, supporting audience evidence and usage permissions. No creator is currently selected or committed.

The first agreement should define the pilot’s scope, fees, expenses, approval turnaround, content ownership/permissions, reporting and stop/go decision. The next stage can be agreed after reviewing actual evidence.
